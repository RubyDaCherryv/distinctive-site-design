## 1. Site types

For hybrids, take the page job from the dominant goal and borrow one device from the secondary type.

### Sales landing (продающий сайт)
- **Job**: move a visitor to one action (buy, book, sign up, request a quote).
- **Sequence**: promise, proof, how it works, objection handling, offer, action. Order encodes persuasion, so this is one place where a real sequence exists.
- **Hero thesis**: the outcome the customer gets, shown concretely (the product in use, a before/after, a live calculator), plus one primary action.
- **Density**: medium to high; scannable; repeated primary action; visible price or next step.
- **Motion**: functional (reveals that pace the argument, feedback on the action). Never delay the action.
- **Risks**: template feature grids, vague claims, several competing calls to action, decoration that slows the path.
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Instrument/technical, Craft and material, Swiss clarity, Playful and tactile, Documentary.

### Image / brand page (имиджевая страница)
- **Job**: build desire and recognition; make the brand feel like something.
- **Sequence**: mood first, then a few chapters that reveal the brand's world; fewer sections, more air, stronger pacing. Action is soft (contact, follow).
- **Hero thesis**: one image, film-like moment, or typographic statement with a clear point of view.
- **Density**: low; large type and imagery; rhythm of full-bleed and quiet sections.
- **Motion**: atmospheric and choreographed (scroll-driven reveals, parallax with restraint).
- **Risks**: generic stock imagery, empty grandeur with no content, motion that hurts performance or accessibility.
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Cinematic, Editorial print, Soft organic, Retro-future, Brutalist raw.

### Business card / portfolio (сайт-визитка)
- **Job**: say who, what, proof, how to reach, fast.
- **Sequence**: identity, what I do, selected work or proof, contact. One screen of substance before any scroll if possible.
- **Hero thesis**: the person's or studio's name and craft, expressed through the type and one distinctive detail; contact one tap away.
- **Density**: low to medium; quick to scan on a phone; contact details always reachable.
- **Motion**: minimal, personality-driven micro-interactions.
- **Risks**: bio walls, skills as progress bars, identical project cards without context.
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Swiss clarity, Editorial print, Brutalist raw, Playful and tactile, Documentary.

### Event / campaign
- **Job**: date, place, why go, register.
- **Sequence**: what and when, lineup or program, venue and logistics, register.
- **Hero thesis**: the event's date and place as design material (a poster-like composition).
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Poster and print, Retro-future, Brutalist raw, Playful and tactile.

### Product showcase
- **Job**: help someone understand a product's shape and use.
- **Sequence**: the object, what it does, details up close, specs, where to get it.
- **Hero thesis**: the product itself, large, or an interactive demo.
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Instrument/technical, Cinematic, Craft and material, Swiss clarity.

### Editorial / content
- **Job**: reading comfort and navigation for text-heavy content.
- **Hero thesis**: the strongest headline or image of the piece.
- **Directions that tend to fit**: Editorial print, Documentary, Swiss clarity.

## 2. Direction library

Each direction is a family of traits, not a template. Push it toward the subject's own materials and vocabulary. Avoid the three defaults listed in `design-principles.md` unless the brief asks for them.

| Direction | Traits | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument / technical | Data-forward, monospace or grotesque utility type, grids, readouts, precise alignment, diagram-like details | Products with real specs, engineering, science, tools | The subject is emotional or handmade |
| Craft and material | Textures, tactile surfaces, hand-made marks, close photography, generous detail (palette comes from the real materials, not from a default warm cream) | Food, ceramics, furniture, small makers | Fast-scan sales pages that need clean hierarchy |
| Swiss clarity | Strict grid, one or two type families, strong scale contrast, restrained color, functional layout | Portfolios, B2B, institutions, clear information | Brands that need warmth or theatre |
| Editorial print | Large expressive type, asymmetric columns, pull quotes, image-and-text collage | Publishing, culture, fashion-adjacent, long stories | Transaction-heavy flows |
| Cinematic | Full-bleed imagery or video, dark-to-light passages, big quiet type, slow pacing | Film, hospitality, premium products, image pages | Low-bandwidth audiences, text-heavy subjects |
| Soft organic | Rounded forms, layered soft color fields, fluid shapes, gentle motion | Wellness, care, nature, education for kids | Precision or industrial subjects |
| Retro-future | Period-specific graphic language (scan lines, chrome, grain, early-web or space-age motifs) used with wit | Games, music, events, tech nostalgia | Serious institutions |
| Brutalist raw | Exposed structure, heavy type, harsh contrast, deliberate roughness | Studios, art, counter-culture, events | Audiences who need reassurance |
| Playful and tactile | Bold flat color, chunky shapes, oversized controls, delight in interaction | Consumer apps, food, kids, community | Sober financial or medical contexts |
| Documentary | Photo-led, captions, real names and places, plain honest type | NGOs, journalism, local businesses with real stories | Abstract SaaS with no imagery |
| Poster and print | Single composed sheet, poster hierarchy, overprint and cut-paper moves | Events, launches, cultural programs | Multi-step conversion flows |

## 3. Matching rules

1. Start from the subject's own world: its materials, instruments, artifacts, vocabulary. A direction should be justified by something in that list.
2. Then check the goal: does the direction support the page job (a cinematic direction can hurt a fast-scan sales page)?
3. Then check the audience's context: device, bandwidth, formality, reading habits. Include phone and foldable use.
4. If two directions tie, choose the one with fewer templated associations for this subject.
5. Mix at most two directions and name which one leads.
6. Record the choice in one line: `Direction: <lead> (+ <accent>), because <subject fact>; job: <page job>.`

## 4. Worked example (short)

Subject: a small hand-roasted coffee subscription. Job: sell a subscription (sales landing).
Candidates: (a) Craft and material: fits real beans, paper bags, roast logs; risk: slower scanning. (b) Instrument/technical: roast curves as hero; risk: cold for a consumer product. (c) Playful and tactile: fits subscription frequency selector; risk: less premium.
Choice: Craft and material leads, with one instrument-style detail: a live roast-curve graphic as the signature, because roast profile is the real product difference. Sequence follows the sales structure above.
